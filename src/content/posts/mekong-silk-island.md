## Mekong Silk Island: Where Tradition Meets Tranquility

Just a short boat ride from the bustling streets of Phnom Penh lies **Mekong Silk Island (Koh Dach)**—a peaceful retreat where time seems to slow down and Cambodia’s weaving heritage comes alive.

  src="https://cdn.cambopedia.com/img/R0002042.jpg"

Known for its centuries-old silk weaving tradition, Silk Island is home to skilled artisans who transform delicate threads into vibrant fabrics using traditional wooden looms. 

<Image
  src="https://cdn.cambopedia.com/img/R0002090.jpg"
  alt="Koh Dach in pictures"
  width={1920}
  height={1280}
  priority={false}
/>
Walking through the villages, you’ll hear the rhythmic sounds of weaving and see colorful scarves, sampots, and textiles drying in the sun—each piece telling a story of culture, patience, and craftsmanship.


<Image
  src="https://cdn.cambopedia.com/img/R0002097.jpg"
  alt="Koh Dach in pictures"
  width={1920}
  height={1280}
  priority={false}
/>

Beyond weaving, the island offers a glimpse into authentic rural life along the Mekong River. Quiet roads, stilted wooden houses, friendly locals, and lush gardens create an atmosphere that feels worlds away from the city. Whether you explore by bicycle, visit family-run workshops, or simply enjoy the river breeze, Silk Island invites you to experience Cambodia at a gentler pace.

A rare perspective of Phnom Penh from the Koh Oknha Tey side. The distance between Koh Oknha Tey and Koh Dach (Silk Island) is roughly 6–8 kilometers in a straight line across the Mekong River area.

Mekong Silk Island is more than a destination—it’s a living museum of tradition, creativity, and community. For travelers seeking culture, calm, and connection, this hidden gem on the Mekong is a journey worth taking.
